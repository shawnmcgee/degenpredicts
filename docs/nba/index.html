This model does not beat the NBA closing line. Out of sample across 7 seasons (2019-20 to 2025-26, 8915 closing lines) its spread error was 10.49 points against the close's 10.494 and its totals error 14.329 against 14.329; its side of the spread covered 51.1% of 8769 games (±0.53), short of the 52.4% a −110 price needs. Its spread number is a hair more accurate than the close's — a paired test puts it 2.14σ ahead on the same games — but a hair is all it is, and it does not clear the vig. The published number is therefore the line moved 40% of the way toward the model on spreads and 5% on totals — and the market-aware model seldom sits a point from the line, so that is a few tenths at most. Ratings, injuries and schedule alone — no market — miss by 10.71 and 14.607: that gap is what the line knows and a public box-score model does not.

On the moneyline the model's win probabilities scored a log loss of 0.6056 against the de-vigged closing price's 0.6058 — a dead heat. The one place it showed anything is where a moneyline is staked here, at 10%+ EV — nearly always an underdog the closing spread rated better than its price did: +15.9% on 782 bets (±8.8), positive in 5 of 7 seasons. Suggestive, not proven: a bucket needs 2.23σ to survive the looks taken, and the prices behind it are one archive's closing numbers. Closing-line value will settle it. Ours is the published view: the market's line moved toward the model by the amount the backtest earned, which is very little. Ratings alone is the model without the market — team ratings, who is playing, rest and travel. ours % is the chance the pick wins (net of a push on a whole-number line), market % the same chance from the de-vigged price, and EV the expected return per unit at the posted price. A spread or total is flagged play only at 3.0 points of disagreement on the spread or 5.0 on the total, a moneyline at 10.0% EV — and only once both teams have played enough games to rate and no unresolved injury could still move the number by 1.5 points or more. Until then a pick that clears the bar is marked waiting on news. The numbers after each name are the points a game that player is worth over whoever takes his minutes. Prices are from ESPN's partner book.
