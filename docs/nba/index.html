This model does not beat the NBA closing line. Out of sample across 7 seasons (2019-20 to 2025-26, 8915 closing lines) its spread error was 10.494 points against the close's 10.494 and its totals error 14.33 against 14.329; its side of the spread covered 50.4% of 8769 games (±0.53), short of the 52.4% a −110 price needs. The published number is therefore the line moved 35% of the way toward the model on spreads and 0% on totals — and the market-aware model seldom sits a point from the line, so that is a few tenths at most. Ratings, injuries and schedule alone — no market — miss by 10.743 and 14.626: that gap is what the line knows and a public box-score model does not.

On the moneyline the model's win probabilities scored a log loss of 0.6057 against the de-vigged closing price's 0.6058 — a dead heat. The one place it showed anything is where a moneyline is staked here, at 10%+ EV — nearly always an underdog the closing spread rated better than its price did: +16.3% on 773 bets (±8.91), positive in 6 of 7 seasons. Suggestive, not proven: a bucket needs 2.23σ to survive the looks taken, and the prices behind it are one archive's closing numbers. Closing-line value will settle it. Every game is still predicted, graded and CLV-tracked; the numbers below are worth tracking rather than backing.
