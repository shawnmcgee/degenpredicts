This model does not beat the NBA closing line. Out of sample across 7 seasons (2019-20 to 2025-26, 8915 closing lines) its spread error was 10.49 points against the close's 10.494 and its totals error 14.329 against 14.329; its side of the spread covered 51.1% of 8769 games (±0.53), short of the 52.4% a −110 price needs. Its spread number is a hair more accurate than the close's — a paired test puts it 2.14σ ahead on the same games — but a hair is all it is, and it does not clear the vig. The published number is therefore the line moved 40% of the way toward the model on spreads and 5% on totals — and the market-aware model seldom sits a point from the line, so that is a few tenths at most. Ratings, injuries and schedule alone — no market — miss by 10.71 and 14.607: that gap is what the line knows and a public box-score model does not.

On the moneyline the model's win probabilities scored a log loss of 0.6056 against the de-vigged closing price's 0.6058 — a dead heat. The one place it showed anything is where a moneyline is staked here, at 10%+ EV — nearly always an underdog the closing spread rated better than its price did: +15.9% on 782 bets (±8.8), positive in 5 of 7 seasons. Suggestive, not proven: a bucket needs 2.23σ to survive the looks taken, and the prices behind it are one archive's closing numbers. Closing-line value will settle it. How to read a card. Line is the sportsbook's number. Our model is the same game after adjusting for team ratings, injuries, rest and travel. A spread or total becomes a play only when our model is at least 3.0 points off the spread or 5.0 off the total — smaller gaps have never beaten the book in seven seasons of testing — and a moneyline only at 10.0% expected value. Our chance is how often we expect the pick to win, next to the book's chance from its own price (with the vig taken out); EV is the expected return per dollar at the posted price. A pick that clears the bar waits (waiting on news) until both teams have played enough games to rate and no unresolved injury could still move it 1.5 points. The number after each injured player is roughly how many points a game he's worth. Prices are from ESPN's partner book.
