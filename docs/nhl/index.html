The honest backtest. With nothing fitted on the seasons it scores (2023–2025, priced at the noon lines this board bets into), every positive-EV puck-line side returned +5.8% on 767 bets (±3.19), positive in 3 of 3 seasons, and the staking rule +12.5% on 153. Suggestive, not proven: a slice needs |z| ≥ 2.86 to survive the number of looks taken. Closing-line value is what will settle it.

Against closing moneylines the model tied the market (log-loss difference under 0.0001); against noon prices it was better by 0.0006. On totals the market was as good as the model (log loss 0.6925 against 0.6926), so the published total sits 30% of the way from the market to the model and almost never clears the bar. The full walk-forward reads higher — +14.5% on 379 — because its late-game model and shrinks were fitted on overlapping seasons; the number above is the one to trust.

Goalies. From recent starts alone the model names the right starter 65.0% of the time. Knowing the real one sharpens its ratings-only forecast (moneyline log loss 0.0003 better) but barely moves the market-aware one against closing lines (+0.0001): the books price the starter too. So the board takes the starter from the day's news and does not stake a pick until both are confirmed or likely — it is never betting blind into goalie news.

The bar is our probability of each result after 60 minutes. ours is the chance the pick wins (a total's is net of a push on a whole-number line), market is the same chance from the de-vigged price, and EV is the expected return per unit at the posted price. Goals is our expected final total, empty-netters and the shootout goal included, and market is the same number read from the market's prices through the same model — compare those two, not our goals with the posted line. A 5.5 or a 6.0 at even money already expects about 5.9 or 6.2 goals, so against the line most games would look like an over. A pick is flagged play only at EV of 3.0% or more on the puck line and 5.0% on the total, once both teams have played enough games to rate — and only once both starting goalies are confirmed or likely. Until then a pick that clears the bar is marked waiting on goalies. Starters come from the day's news; with none yet, the model uses its own guess from recent starts and the back-to-back rule, and says how sure it is. Every goalie carries his own rating, which follows him through trades.

The last few minutes of a close game decide the puck line: a team down one or two chases, pulls its goalie, and either ties it or gives up an empty-netter. in regulation a plain Poisson model puts 30% of games on a one-goal margin, and the real figure is 18% — the rest get tied up or iced late. This model plays out the final ten minutes goal by goal.
